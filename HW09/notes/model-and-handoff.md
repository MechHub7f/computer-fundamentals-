# Model construction, sensitivity, and handoff analysis

## Purpose and provenance

The user authorized an informal alternate-history scenario after being told that the historical evidence did not establish a complete EIC-versus-VOC return comparison. The paper now distinguishes its invented British financial assumptions from sourced historical context in one prominent opening notice. It is not a verified reconstruction of an actual investment made in January 1725. The notice must travel with the paper and PDF. The accompanying numerical model is useful for explaining total returns, demonstrating how assumptions influence conclusions, and identifying what a historical study would need next.

The Dutch financial file is Wim Van Lent's 2018 Harvard Dataverse dataset, DOI 10.7910/DVN/S4HNVJ, file ID 3211809. Both the table and the file's XML metadata have been downloaded. The metadata identifies the author, title, year, version, and 94 annual observations from 1679 through 1772. Its field labels are not a complete methodological manual. In particular, a numerical field called DivPct does not by itself settle precisely when a shareholder became entitled to a payment or when money reached that shareholder.

The calculation uses the fields Year, DivPct, and avstockcourseVOCAMS. It deliberately does not use Dividendspayable as a cash receipt by an individual investor. The latter is a seperate aggregate accounting quantity, not an interchangeable dividend-per-share series. This distinction prevents mixing balance-sheet obligations with annual investor cash flow. Similarly, company income, cargo tonnage, and market capitalization cannot simply replace investment returns.

## British assumptions and the terminal comparison

Every British financial number in the model is invented, including the purchase price of 150 for 100 nominal units, eight units of annual distributions, and the sale prices for the six candidate years. These are choices for an illustration, not estimates supported by contemporaneous records. The imagined distributions run from 1726 through 1750, deliberately excluding any payment in the purchase year. This makes the payment count explicit: twenty distributions are included at the 1745 exit and twenty-five at the 1750 exit. A real study must determine the actual dates and entitlements rather than adopt this convenient convention.

At the final exit the British holding returns 200 in sale proceeds plus 200 in retained dividends, for terminal wealth of 400. Subtracting the initial cost of 150 gives profit of 250. The ratio of profit to cost is 1.6667, or about 166.7 percent. This is a cumulative holding-period return, not an annual rate. Calling it an annual rate would drastically exaggerate performance. Nothing here implies that the investor could repeat that gain every year.

The Dutch calculation treats annual prices as percentages of nominal capital. It uses 642.58 as the opening price proxy and approximately 557.3913 as the final price proxy. The sum of annual DivPct entries from 1726 through 1750 is 4.90. Multiplying by a nominal holding of 100 gives 490 in included distributions. Price plus dividends equals approximately 1,047.3913. Relative to the initial cost, the modeled cumulative return is approximately 62.9978 percent. The observed annual averages are genuine dataset entries; treating them as prices at which this ancestor can trade at the specified dates is a modeling assumption.

## Why the model chooses 1750

The exit calculation is repeated separately for 1745, 1746, 1747, 1748, 1749, and 1750. Each candidate includes only the model's distributions through its own year, never the full twenty-five years of dividends. Comparing a 1745 sale plus 1750 dividends would give an investor money from a holding already sold. The British sale path is 170, 160, 165, 180, 190, and 200. The modeled drop in 1746 is part of the invented path; the real capture of Madras does not validate this particular price drop or its magnitude.

Both modeled series achieve their highest terminal wealth at the 1750 candidate. That is a discrete maximum within six choices. It establishes neither a best trading day nor even a best historical year-end quotation, because the Dutch inputs are annual means and the British path is constructed. Buying or selling exactly at a whole-year mean cannot be assumed feasible. Calling the table a historical backtest would obscure this central limitation.

Comparing percentage returns is a way to normalize different starting costs, not a way to eliminate currency risk. The paper assumes exchange rates do not change. For a real ancestor holding wealth in sterling, guilder dividends and sale proceeds need a specified conversion policy. Converting each dividend on receipt is different from accumulating guilders and converting everything at the exit date. Interest on accumulated cash is also omitted. These simplifying conventions are legitimate in a stated scenario but must not become unacknowledged historical facts.

## Sensitivity and evidence that could reverse the result

The result is constructed, not robust empirical evidence. For example, holding the final British proceeds and cumulative dividends at 400, increasing its purchase cost from 150 to 250 reduces its cumulative return to 60 percent. That would fall slightly below the modeled VOC result of 63 percent. Thus an uncertain initial valuation can reverse the recommendation without changing the final sale price at all. The break-even purchase price against the VOC proxy is approximately 245.40. This threshold is conditional on all the other model choices, not a prediction about a real quotation.

The result is also sensitive to distributions. With a British purchase cost of 150 and final sale value of 200, the holding needs cumulative dividends above approximately 44.50 to exceed the modeled VOC terminal multiple. Spread equally across twenty-five years, that is about 1.78 per year for 100 nominal units. Neither threshold proves the company actually paid the necessary amount. They identify useful questions to ask of missing records.

There is already qualitative counterevidence to simplistic recommendations. The Dutch proxy produces a positive total return despite a lower final price, so declining stock prices alone do not prove that VOC ownership was unprofitable. The British loss of Madras demonstrates vulnerability within the period. The St Helena records contradict any claim that choosing British shares avoids slavery. Conversely, criticism of particular executions by the Heren XVII does not establish that the VOC ceased coercive colonial practices. These are constraints on interpretation, not a completed post-draft adversarial web-search exercise.

## Further notes from the source texts

These are additional observations from extracted text, not a claim that the student has checked the scanned pages. Bibliography identifiers refer to the linked entries in the paper. OCR errors, translation errors, and omitted context remain possible. Each proposed use should be checked against the page image and original language before being relied on in a historical paper.

### Banda and the Dutch description [P01]

The 1739 work describes a 1609 agreement involving local leaders, deliveries of nutmeg at a specified price, and company protection. Its account then says the company used arms to compel Banda in 1621 and obtained control of the islands. Page 472 describes dividing lands into plots for settlers and having those lands worked by enslaved people. It states that the settlers delivered nutmeg and mace to company employees. Reading the previous page as well as the slavery sentence shows that the description relates production and delivery to a history of military domination and control over land.

The account uses a company-centered perspective: it describes local deliveries to other traders as a failure to keep an agreement and frames the company's armed intervention as a response. Those descriptions are the author's claims and should not be adopted as a complete neutral history of the conquest. The passage is still valuable as a contemporary description of labor and company deliveries. It does not identify exactly how much a 1725 shareholder earned from forced work, nor does it give a complete accounting of every VOC post. The historical events described on page 471 precede the investment, while the 1739 publication itself falls within the holding period.

### St Helena and the EIC [P05]

The 1725 letter summarized on page 169 says that free Black people were not allowed to own enslaved people and that a Black person enfranchised by a master should leave the island on the first available ship. These are seperate rules. The second is the more relevant evidence of a restriction placed on a person's freedom of movement after manumission. Neither provision should be paraphrased to suggest that all free Black residents were automatically expelled, because the cited wording is more specific.

The 1727 entry immediately below reports a governor's concern about spending £3,202 on beef and pork for the company's Black workers over 1723–1726, and a proposal to save money by employing them to fish. The excerpt shows cost-conscious management, but the entry alone should not be treated as a shareholder dividend account. The 1739 entry on page 182 describes nine Company-owned enslaved people, men, women and children recently received from Bombay, escaping at night in a longboat. The compiler reproduces the authorities' account, including its characterization of an alleged instigator; the people's own voices and reasons for flight are not directly preserved in this excerpt.

The island examples concern a particular part of the company's operations, not necessarily every British trading post. Yet they are directly relevant to an ethical standard that rejects ownership of people and forced labor. Comparing individual documented abuses against a complete institutional history of the VOC would be uneven; both sides require comparable scope and context.

### Batavia, amnesty, and editorial context [P02]

The extracted 1740 amnesty describes displaced Chinese people, presents a one-month period for returning to company obedience, and promises protection to certain people who comply. It also excludes alleged leaders and discusses rewards for their capture. This mixture of amnesty, demands for submission, and punitive exceptions is important: a document that offers protection can simultaneously demonstrate coercive administrative power. Its explanation of who caused events is the government's explanation, not an independent verdict about all participants.

The 1887 editor moves between primary text and historical commentary. Immediately before quoting the 1742 directors' letter, the editor describes authorization of summary proceedings and executions of 41 Chinese people without sworn proof of guilt. The quotation then criticizes the practice as resembling murder rather than justice. Those 41 cases are an editor's account preceding a quotation from the period letter; the editor's statement must not be falsely presented as the exact words of the directors. Before quoting the original Dutch in a final paper, read the scan to correct OCR and confirm attribution and surrounding pages.

### Madras and market data [S02, S05]

Mentz explains that the 1746 French occupation of Madras involved competing expectations about ransom and property, and that the 1748 treaty restored the town after damage and expense. His interpretation identifies divergent interests among the East India Company, private merchants, French officials, and British authorities. A researcher can use this to discuss business risk during the investment horizon, but cannot infer a stock price or investor return from the event alone. Evidence of losses at a settlement is not automatically evidence of the change in a company's quoted market value.

Beach, Norman, and Wills state in the caption to figure 7, page 71, that EIC ex-dividend periods in 1724–1725 began around the middle of January and July, changing from 1726 onward. The exact purchase date therefore affects both the price quotation and whether the buyer receives the forthcoming distribution. Their work compares London and Amsterdam prices for British securities and examines spot versus forward quotation conventions. A careful historical calculation should identify the exchange and settlement basis instead of combining quotes with no adjustment. The paper's six scenario exit years cannot substitute for those real observations.

### Check before formal use

The source text confirms why the paper's ethical discussion requires more than selecting whichever company has the better modeled numbers. Both sets of records indicate coercive practices, but they are not a common statistical measure of all harms. The 1740s VOC violence and the 1720s–1730s St Helena records concern different places, types of evidence, and groups of affected people. A forced-choice ethical ranking would need a stated metric and evidence of similar scope for both companies. Declining to rank them is a meaningful conclusion when the comparison is not supported.

For the research-paper assignment, the next factual steps are to obtain actual January 1725 EIC and VOC quotations, establish dividend entitlements and payment dates, compare actual exit quotations in 1745–1750, and document currency assumptions. The student should then read the original cited pages and a translation where needed, seek sources that challenge the resulting conclusion, and revise. The current scenario's arithmetic demonstrates a method but does not supply any of those missing observations.
